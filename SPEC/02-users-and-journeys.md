# Users and Service Journeys

## Claimant

A claimant needs to start a report after an accident, theft, or loss without first learning insurance terminology or internal process steps.

The claimant must be able to:

- describe the event in their own language;
- see, confirm, and correct the system's structured understanding;
- provide images or documents and understand their status;
- know what has happened, what is missing, why it matters, who acts next, and the expected timing;
- continue after several days without repeating the report;
- obtain suitable human support for urgency, difficulty, accessibility, distress, or preference;
- avoid repeating confirmed information after handoff.

## Claims Professional

A claims professional needs the claimant's confirmed account, field sources, evidence, gaps, conflicts, internal signals, handoff reason, and requested decision or action. Their time should be used for support, judgement, and exceptions rather than reconstructing the intake.

## Claims Operations

Claims operations owns service flow, governance, quality, and operating efficiency. It needs consistent routing, traceability, measurable effort, and a workbench that reflects live claim state rather than a second manually maintained record.

## Service Journey

1. The claimant starts or resumes a report.
2. The agent accepts a natural-language account and available evidence.
3. The system proposes structured facts with source and confidence.
4. The claimant confirms or corrects material facts.
5. The system retrieves relevant policy, history, and evidence state.
6. Explicit rules and the agent select the next safe action.
7. The system asks, clarifies, proceeds, updates, creates, or hands off.
8. The claimant and staff receive role-appropriate status and next steps.

## Experience Principle

Users should choose the agent because it is immediate, adaptive, transparent, resumable, and able to preserve context across digital and human service. Adoption must come from competent behaviour and user control, not claims of model intelligence or universal superiority over phone and forms.

## Research Boundary

Current user needs include challenge facts, team decisions, and hypotheses that still require evidence. Public process material, complaints, a claimant survey, and structured insurance-industry input should be traceable through research synthesis before new findings are promoted into this specification.
