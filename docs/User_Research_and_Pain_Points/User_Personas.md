# Insurance FNOL User Personas

## Purpose and Research Basis

This document defines five evidence-informed personas for the First Notice of Loss (FNOL) experience:

- three claimant personas: First-Time Claimant, Efficiency-Seeking Claimant, and Urgent or Complex Claimant;
- two employee personas: Claims Professional and Claims Operations Lead.

The claimant personas were developed from an exploratory user survey, existing FNOL journeys and fields, public customer feedback, digital-claim research, and research into claim verification and fraud controls. The employee personas were developed from secondary research into published insurer intake requirements, operating volumes, surge conditions, and multi-party claim handoffs.

The personas focus on behaviours, goals, needs, roles, and claim situations. Demographic details such as age, occupation, and income are intentionally excluded because the evidence does not support them. Role and situation labels are used instead of fictional personal names.

Current survey values are recalculated from the consented responses and documented in **Appendix A — Claimant Survey Evidence**. Employee personas do not use claimant survey percentages and require direct staff validation.

## Persona Standard

This document applies the following professional persona conventions:

- personas are evidence-informed archetypes, not fictional biographies or representations of individual participants;
- each persona is identified by a role, experience state, or service situation rather than a personal name;
- every persona uses the same structure: Snapshot, Context and Triggers, Goals, Behaviours, Jobs to Be Done, Pain Points, Needs, Desired Outcomes, Key Evidence, and Evidence Confidence;
- observed or reported evidence is distinguished from research inference and desired product outcomes;
- evidence confidence and required validation are stated explicitly;
- claimant survey evidence and employee secondary research are kept separate.

## Appropriate Use of the Personas

These personas are decision aids for understanding different goals and support needs. They are not fixed customer types, eligibility categories, risk scores, or workflow routes.

A person may move between the claimant modes during one claim. For example, an experienced claimant may become an Urgent or Complex Claimant when an injury, dispute, accessibility need, or unusual event occurs. The relevant experience should therefore respond to the person's current situation, confirmed facts, evidence state, comprehension, and support preference rather than permanently assigning a label.

The employee personas describe responsibilities at two professional levels:

- the **Claims Professional** focuses on individual-claim judgement, clarification, review, and progression;
- the **Claims Operations Lead** focuses on queues, service flow, quality, capacity, governance, and operational visibility.

The employee personas are supported by secondary process and operational evidence only. They should guide prototype hypotheses, but detailed workflow, terminology, permissions, and performance measures require validation with authorised staff.

## Persona Overview

| Persona | Role or situation | Core problem | Primary outcome sought |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-Time Claimant** | No previous claim experience | Does not know how to begin or what evidence matters | Complete FNOL correctly with clear guidance |
| **Efficiency-Seeking Claimant** | Previous claim experience | Rework, irrelevant questions, fragmented evidence requests, and unclear progress | Complete FNOL with minimal effort and receive proactive updates |
| **Urgent or Complex Claimant** | Urgent, complex, unusual, vulnerable, or disputed situation | Routine self-service cannot safely handle the case | Reach an authorised human with full context |
| **Claims Professional** | Reviews, clarifies, and progresses individual claims | Intake can be incomplete, inconsistent, or difficult to reconstruct | Receive a complete, traceable, and actionable claim context |
| **Claims Operations Lead** | Manages queues, service flow, quality, and capacity | High and event-driven volumes make prioritisation and coordination difficult | Maintain safe, visible, and scalable claim operations |

The first two claimant personas are segmented by previous claim experience:

- **60.0%** had never made an insurance claim;
- **40.0%** had previously made an insurance claim.

The third is a situational support mode that may apply to either experience segment. The employee personas are role-based and are not derived from claimant survey segmentation.

## Persona 1: First-Time Claimant

### Snapshot

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| **Archetype type** | Experience-based claimant archetype |
| **Survey segment** | 60.0% had never made an insurance claim |
| **Typical situation** | A minor vehicle accident or another insured event has just occurred |
| **Process familiarity** | Low; unfamiliar with the correct sequence, terminology, and evidence requirements |
| **Emotional state** | Anxious, uncertain, and concerned about making a mistake |
| **Primary goal** | Understand what to do immediately and complete the first report correctly |
| **Main barrier** | Does not know which actions, information, and evidence are required |

### Context and Triggers

- An insured event has occurred for the first time.
- The user may still be at the scene and managing safety, police contact, towing, or third parties.
- Important evidence may be time-sensitive.
- The user does not naturally organise the incident into insurer-defined fields.

### Goals

- **Functional:** Take the correct immediate actions and submit a complete initial report.
- **Emotional:** Feel reassured that inexperience will not cause a damaging mistake.
- **Service:** Understand what is required now, what can wait, and what happens next.

### Behaviours

- Searches for immediate instructions before starting a full claim form.
- Worries about losing evidence that cannot be collected later.
- May switch between website, app, chat, phone, or in-person support for reassurance.
- Relies on explicit confirmation that each important step is complete.

### Jobs to Be Done

> When an insured incident happens for the first time, I want the service to explain what I should do and ask only questions relevant to my situation, so I do not miss an important step because I lack experience.

### Pain Points

1. Does not know whether to focus first on safety, evidence, police contact, or reporting.
2. Does not know which photos, documents, or third-party details are required.
3. Finds insurance terminology and coverage difficult to interpret.
4. Cannot distinguish relevant questions from fields intended for other claim types.
5. Remains unsure after submission whether enough information was provided.
6. Worries that AI may misunderstand an unstructured description.

### Needs

- Situation-specific safety and evidence guidance.
- A clear distinction between information required now and information that can be added later.
- Plain-language explanations connected to the incident.
- A way to verify and correct how the incident has been understood.
- Clear acknowledgement, missing-information status, ownership, and next steps.
- Accessible human support throughout the journey.

### Desired Outcomes

- Knows the immediate next action and which evidence is time-sensitive.
- Can complete FNOL without prior process knowledge.
- Can verify the structured claim information before submission.
- Knows whether submission is complete and what happens next.

### Key Evidence

The strongest signals are uncertainty about immediate action (75.0%), evidence collection (66.7%), possible coverage (50.0%), and process duration (41.7%). See Appendix A.4 for the current source table.

### Evidence Confidence

**Medium.** Supported by the no-previous-claim survey branch and wider process research, but the survey is small and exploratory. Validate through interviews and first-use usability testing.

## Persona 2: Efficiency-Seeking Claimant

### Snapshot

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| **Archetype type** | Experience-based claimant archetype |
| **Survey segment** | 40.0% had previously made an insurance claim |
| **Typical situation** | Understands the basic idea of claiming but does not want another slow, fragmented process |
| **Process familiarity** | Moderate; familiar with reporting and evidence but not every product or internal workflow |
| **Emotional state** | Impatient with unnecessary effort and concerned about repeated follow-up |
| **Primary goal** | Complete the initial report once and receive proactive progress information |
| **Main barrier** | Repetition, irrelevant questions, fragmented document requests, and unclear status |

### Context and Triggers

- The insurer may already hold customer, policy, and contact information.
- A new claim type can still create uncertainty despite previous experience.
- The user may move between online forms, phone, app, and email.
- The user expects prior information and completed work to be reused.

### Goals

- **Functional:** Complete the report with minimal rework and provide all currently required evidence.
- **Emotional:** Feel that time is respected and the insurer is managing the process.
- **Service:** Receive proactive status, ownership, timing, and delay information.

### Behaviours

- Tries to complete reporting in one interaction.
- Expects known policy and customer data to be pre-existing.
- Becomes frustrated by irrelevant questions and duplicate requests.
- Contacts the insurer when progress is not visible.

### Jobs to Be Done

> When I make another claim, I want the service to reuse existing information, ask only relevant questions, and proactively communicate progress, so I can complete the report with minimal rework.

### Pain Points

1. Each claim feels as if it starts from zero.
2. Static forms include questions that do not apply.
3. Evidence requirements become clear only after submission.
4. Different channels or staff request the same information again.
5. Expected timing, current owner, and next action are not visible.
6. Updates depend on the claimant contacting the insurer.
7. Terminology and decision explanations remain difficult.

### Needs

- Reuse of existing customer, policy, and contact information.
- Questions that adapt to claim type and previous answers.
- A consolidated completeness and evidence view.
- Visible receipt status for uploaded evidence.
- One continuous claim context across channels and staff.
- Proactive progress, timing, ownership, and delay updates.
- Plain-language explanations connected to facts and policy requirements.

### Desired Outcomes

- Completes the initial report without avoidable repetition.
- Understands whether the FNOL is complete before submission.
- Does not need to chase routine status information.
- Can always see the current owner, next action, and expected timing.

### Key Evidence

Strong signals include evidence uncertainty (62.5%), unknown process duration (62.5%), unknown claim status (62.5%), uncertainty about information sufficiency (50.0%), and having to contact the insurer for updates (50.0%). See Appendix A.5 for the current tables and response distributions.

### Evidence Confidence

**Low to Medium.** The previous-claim branch is especially small. The results support preliminary needs and behaviours, not prevalence estimates. Validate through interviews and journey testing.

## Persona 3: Urgent or Complex Claimant

### Snapshot

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| **Archetype type** | Situational claimant support archetype that may overlap with either experience segment |
| **Typical situation** | Serious accident, injury, uninhabitable property, multi-party event, evidence conflict, or dispute |
| **Process familiarity** | Variable; complexity may exceed self-service capability regardless of experience |
| **Emotional state** | Highly stressed, vulnerable, and concerned about safety or major financial impact |
| **Primary goal** | Reach an authorised human who receives the full context and takes responsibility |
| **Main barrier** | Routine automation struggles with exceptions and handoffs may restart the interaction |

### Context and Triggers

- **Urgency:** injury, towing, emergency repair, temporary accommodation, or continuing loss.
- **Complexity:** multiple vehicles, third parties, witnesses, police, or insurers.
- **Exception:** the incident does not fit available options or rules.
- **Dispute:** liability, coverage, exclusion, evidence, valuation, fraud, or non-disclosure concern.
- **Vulnerability:** high stress, language or accessibility need, or inability to complete a digital process.
- **High impact:** error or delay could create serious safety, financial, or legal consequences.

### Goals

- **Functional:** Reach the correct authorised team without restarting the report.
- **Emotional:** Trust that a responsible person is handling high-impact decisions.
- **Service:** Retain context, evidence, ownership, and expected response time through escalation.

### Behaviours

- Seeks human support earlier than a routine claimant.
- May struggle to fit the incident into standard form options.
- Needs reassurance that urgent circumstances have been recognised.
- May challenge or correct automated interpretations and decisions.

### Jobs to Be Done

> When my claim is urgent, complex, or disputed, I want the system to recognise the risk and transfer me to the correct human team with my conversation, information, evidence, and escalation reason, so the claim can continue without restarting.

### Pain Points

1. Routine digital questions do not reflect the seriousness or complexity of the incident.
2. AI may misunderstand the event and continue with incorrect information.
3. The user cannot see when or how a human becomes involved.
4. Automation may enter areas requiring human judgement.
5. Conversation, fields, evidence, and escalation reason may not reach the human.
6. Responsibility becomes fragmented across staff and specialists.
7. Verification or dispute decisions lack specific explanations and correction paths.
8. Repeating a distressing event increases emotional burden.

### Needs

- Early recognition of safety, urgency, complexity, dispute, and vulnerability.
- A visible human-support path at every stage.
- Correct routing to an authorised team or specialist.
- Full transfer of confirmed facts, evidence, gaps, and escalation reason.
- Clear ownership and expected response time.
- Human oversight of liability, coverage, fraud, denial, and other high-impact decisions.
- Correction, explanation, review, and complaint pathways.

### Desired Outcomes

- Serious or complex signals are recognised early.
- The user reaches a suitable human without repeating confirmed information.
- Ownership and response timing remain visible.
- High-impact decisions include human oversight and an explanation or review path.

### Key Evidence

Human support is preferred after a serious or stressful incident (65.0%), for a complex or unusual situation (55.0%), when required information is not understood (45.0%), and when there is disagreement (45.0%). See Appendix A.6 for the current source table.

### Evidence Confidence

**Medium as a situational support mode.** Supported by claimant responses and external handoff research, but it is not a mutually exclusive population segment. Validate through complex-journey testing and interviews.

## Persona 4: Claims Professional

### Snapshot

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| **Archetype type** | Role-based employee archetype responsible for reviewing and progressing individual claims |
| **Typical situation** | Receives a new, incomplete, conflicting, urgent, or escalated FNOL |
| **Process familiarity** | High, but dependent on the quality and traceability of incoming information |
| **Emotional state** | Focused on accuracy and service; pressured when context must be reconstructed |
| **Primary goal** | Understand the claim quickly and take the correct next action |
| **Main barrier** | Information, evidence, sources, gaps, and requested action may be fragmented |

### Context and Triggers

- Claim requirements vary across vehicle, property, contents, business, travel, and other products.
- Incoming information may include narrative, structured fields, documents, external-provider input, and AI proposals.
- The claim may require clarification, professional judgement, or handoff to another party.
- Confirmed facts must be distinguished from missing, conflicting, or low-confidence information.

### Goals

- **Functional:** Review a complete and traceable claim context and perform the next authorised action.
- **Emotional:** Trust the information without losing professional control or accountability.
- **Service:** Avoid asking customers to repeat information that is already confirmed.

### Behaviours

- Checks completeness, consistency, evidence, and policy relevance.
- Reconstructs the incident when information is fragmented.
- Clarifies gaps or conflicts before progressing high-impact actions.
- Coordinates with claimants, operations staff, repairers, assessors, and specialists.

### Jobs to Be Done

> When I receive a claim or escalation, I want one traceable view of the confirmed account, evidence, gaps, conflicts, and requested action, so I can apply judgement and progress the claim without rebuilding the intake.

### Pain Points

1. Intake requirements are information-intensive and claim-specific.
2. Missing or incorrectly routed information creates follow-up work.
3. Field sources and AI-generated proposals may be difficult to distinguish.
4. Evidence can be disconnected from the fact or decision it supports.
5. Handoffs may not make ownership or the requested action clear.
6. Context reconstruction uses time intended for support and judgement.

### Needs

- A claimant-confirmed incident summary and structured form.
- Field provenance and visible evidence state.
- Separate views of confirmed, missing, pending, conflicting, and low-confidence information.
- A clear handoff reason, priority, ownership, and requested action.
- Prior communication and promised next steps.
- Authority boundaries and human control over high-impact decisions.

### Desired Outcomes

- Understands the claim without repeating confirmed questions.
- Can identify evidence gaps and conflicts immediately.
- Knows why the claim was transferred and what action is required.
- Records staff action once and updates the shared claim state.

### Key Evidence

Published AA Insurance, Tower, and AMI claim guidance demonstrates substantial and claim-specific information requirements. Tower and IFSO material demonstrates routine involvement of repairers, assessors, engineers, builders, and other specialists. These sources support the intake and coordination requirement but do not directly measure employee experience.

### Evidence Confidence

**Medium for operational need; Low for lived experience.** Based on published processes and case evidence rather than staff interviews. Validate through claims-handler interviews, observation, and handling-time data.

## Persona 5: Claims Operations Lead

### Snapshot

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| **Archetype type** | Role-based employee archetype responsible for service flow, queues, capacity, quality, and governance |
| **Typical situation** | Manages routine claim volumes and event-driven surges across teams and external providers |
| **Process familiarity** | High; focused on portfolio-level state rather than one claim narrative |
| **Emotional state** | Accountable for service quality and safety under changing demand |
| **Primary goal** | Direct available capacity to the safest and most valuable next actions |
| **Main barrier** | Volume, urgency, completeness, ownership, and timing may not be visible in one operational view |

### Context and Triggers

- Claim volumes are high and can rise sharply during severe-weather events.
- Digital intake and automation coexist with exception handling and professional review.
- Work is distributed across internal staff, repair networks, assessors, and other specialists.
- Queue order alone does not represent urgency, readiness, risk, or service commitment.

### Goals

- **Functional:** Prioritise, assign, monitor, and improve claim flow across routine and surge demand.
- **Emotional:** Maintain confidence that urgent and high-impact cases are not hidden in the queue.
- **Service:** Deliver consistent ownership, timing, quality, and claimant communication.

### Behaviours

- Monitors queue volume, age, state, priority, assignment, and next action.
- Redistributes work when demand or capacity changes.
- Investigates stalled, repeated, or escalated cases.
- Reviews quality, overrides, exceptions, and operational signals.

### Jobs to Be Done

> When demand changes, I want a live operational view of claim readiness, urgency, ownership, next action, and service timing, so I can allocate capacity and protect service quality without maintaining a second manual record.

### Pain Points

1. High and event-driven volumes create prioritisation pressure.
2. Arrival-order queues do not show readiness, urgency, or required authority.
3. Claim state may be distributed across systems or manually maintained views.
4. External-provider handoffs make ownership and timing harder to monitor.
5. Small amounts of manual work per claim become material at scale.
6. Staff action may not produce a corresponding claimant-facing update.

### Needs

- Live views for urgent, new or untriaged, awaiting-evidence, professional-review, and ready-to-progress cases.
- Filters by state, priority, assignee, next action, and service timing.
- One shared claim state with visible ownership and blockers.
- Traceable assignment, override, and completion activity.
- Capacity and surge visibility across internal and external participants.
- Staff actions that write back to claim state and appropriate customer updates.

### Desired Outcomes

- Urgent and ready-to-progress claims are visible and prioritised.
- Queue ownership, blockers, and service timing are measurable.
- Teams can scale during event-driven demand without losing control of handoffs.
- Operational reporting reflects live claim state rather than a parallel manual record.

### Key Evidence

Published AA and Tower material reports high interaction and claim volumes, peak or severe-weather demand, digital claim adoption, automation, repair networks, and third-party assessment capacity. This supports the need for scalable prioritisation and coordination but does not quantify employee frustration or usability.

### Evidence Confidence

**Medium for operational need; Low for lived experience.** Validate through operations-lead interviews, queue analytics, surge retrospectives, and workbench usability testing.

## Cross-Persona Comparison

| Dimension | First-Time Claimant | Efficiency-Seeking Claimant | Urgent or Complex Claimant | Claims Professional | Claims Operations Lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary focus** | Start correctly | Reduce rework | Reach responsible human support | Progress an individual claim | Manage service flow and capacity |
| **Main uncertainty** | What to do and collect | Whether the process is complete and moving | Whether automation will recognise limits | Which facts are reliable and what action is required | Which work is urgent, ready, blocked, or unowned |
| **Context needed** | Situation-specific guidance | Continuous customer and claim history | Full escalation context | Confirmed facts, sources, evidence, gaps, and conflicts | Live state, ownership, priority, timing, and capacity |
| **Main AI risk** | Misunderstanding the first account | Creating repetition or unnecessary work | Mishandling complexity or making unauthorised decisions | Untraceable proposals or hidden uncertainty | Poorly governed signals or misleading prioritisation |
| **Desired outcome** | Complete FNOL confidently | Complete once and stay informed | Human takes over without restart | Apply judgement without rebuilding intake | Maintain safe and scalable operations |

## Shared Experience Principles

1. Guide before requesting a full claim record.
2. Ask only what is relevant to the claim and current state.
3. Preserve one continuous context across customer, AI, employee, and specialist interactions.
4. Distinguish confirmed facts from proposals, gaps, conflicts, and uncertainty.
5. Make ownership, next action, and expected timing visible to the appropriate role.
6. Design human support as a formal path for urgency, complexity, vulnerability, and dispute.
7. Use AI to assist with guidance, extraction, completeness, and routing while retaining human accountability for high-impact decisions.
8. Keep employee actions and claimant-facing status connected to the same claim state.

## Research Limitations

- The claimant survey sample was small and exploratory; percentages are not population estimates.
- The previous-claim branch was especially limited and supports preliminary persona formation rather than prevalence claims.
- Some claimant questions allowed multiple selections, so percentages are not expected to total 100%.
- The urgent or complex claimant is a situational support mode, not an additional experience-segment percentage.
- Claimant answers are self-reported or hypothetical rather than direct observation of insurer operations.
- AI acceptance was measured on the condition that human support could be requested at any time.
- Employee personas are based on published process and operational evidence rather than direct staff interviews or observation.
- Published operating volumes establish scale and variability but do not quantify employee effort, frustration, or workbench usability.
- Multi-party processes establish a coordination requirement but do not prove that a specific insurer loses information during handoff.
- Further validation should include claimant and employee interviews, workflow observation, usability testing, queue analytics, and competitor FNOL journey testing.

## Summary

> First-time claimants need to know how to begin. Experienced claimants need less rework and proactive updates. Urgent or complex claimants need the right human to take over with full context. Claims Professionals need complete and traceable intake. Claims Operations Leads need visible and scalable control of service flow.

Together, the personas support a hybrid FNOL direction: AI assists with guidance, extraction, completeness, and routing; claimants confirm critical information; employees retain professional control and accountability; and all participants work from one continuous claim context.
