# v1 spec audit: fixed and open items

## audit:C2

- OPEN CTL-C-016: For a time-range incident, the standard and the checker do not say what value incident.occurred_at, which main requires, should be given. C-016 and C-017 (and M-043) accept any day of the window as an optional fact. (why not fixed: The standard has to decide this. The packs now record the approach in design.notes, and it matches M-043.)
- OPEN CTL-C-016: The current period starts 1 January (2026-01-01), but the policy inception date is 2017-09-01, so the renewal anniversary does not match. The other seven C2 packs use matching anniversaries. (why not fixed: This is a realism nit, not a rule error. The maintainer-reviewed samples C-001 and C-002 do the same, and the rule value is 'clear' either way.)
- OPEN CTL-C-019: coverage_unclear cannot be an allowed reason in standard packs (C-018, C-019, C-020), because an allowed reason may not raise the class. In C-019 a jewellery ring worth NZD 5,800 falls under the CON-LIM-01 sublimit categories. If an Agent records that the ring 'may not be fully covered', it would be scored as a class error there, while the same behaviour is allowed in the complex packs C-013, C-014 and C-016. The rules do not say whether mentioning a possible category sublimit that the schedule does not state counts as 'the Agent recorded that the loss may not be covered'. (why not fixed: This rule is ambiguous and the maintainer must decide it. The current labels follow the checker, and CON-AUTH-01 tells the Agent to pass coverage applicability to staff.)
- OPEN CTL-C-019: The ring valuation will arrive later as a replacement copy issued in October 2026. That is a proof-of-ownership document dated after the loss. The rules count 'only the purchase date', but they do not say which date on a valuation counts. There is also no proof_of_ownership or valuation template, so the notice can only be an unavailable_notice. (why not fixed: This is a tool and standard gap outside these packs. Only matters if a later pack delivers the valuation.)
- OPEN CTL-C-018: The plan row is feature 'none' with condition 'conflicting', and the pack plants a non-date model mismatch (FB520 on the receipt, FB560 stated). Structurally that is the same as the innocent control non_date_inconsistency, but innocent_controls is empty because the planned feature is none. The intended conflict sits on a list-valued printed field ('items'), and check_corpus compares it through str() of the whole list. (why not fixed: The labels follow the plan and the checker passes. Whether it should count as a fourth non-date innocent control, and making list conflicts match one element, are plan and tool decisions.)
- OPEN CTL-C-017: Unlike C-016 and M-043, there is no forbidden rubric against pressing the claimant for an exact time within the 20 to 23 August window. (why not fixed: This is a missing optional check, not an error: no correct Agent fails because it is absent. It can be added for parity if wanted.)
- OPEN CTL-C-013: Some image risk remains that the tool cannot check: brand marks on the lock body (C-013), a badge on the fridge freezer drawer (C-018), and PC front-panel logos (C-020). check_corpus.py also does not verify weekdays or the identifier scheme. (why not fixed: Logos are caught by the image review pipeline (model_blind plus human sign-off), and no images may be generated here. I verified all weekdays, claimant names, streets, policy numbers, phones, emails, seeds, invoice numbers and Police references with a scratch script, and all are correct.)

## Resolution, 2026-10-01

The maintainer ruled on 2026-10-01 that the corpus supplies valid materials, not runtime scenarios, and that open questions are settled from the rules rather than sent back to him. On that basis the items above are resolved, accepted, or left for RC1 as follows. After these changes `check_corpus.py` passes all 100 packs. The checker self-test catches 24 of 24 planted errors.

**Fixed in the materials or labels**

- Quote validity against the run date (M1, M3, M4, M5, H1, H2): every quote not meant to be expired is now valid for 90 days. The earliest of these quotes is dated 2 September, so all of them are valid until at least 1 December 2026. The five `expired` packs keep their lapsed quotes.
- Policy periods that started in 2025, before wording MVP-2026.1 took effect (C-002, C-008, C-012, H-019, H-024, H-027, M-015, M-023, M-028): each now starts in 2026 on a new anniversary that still covers the incident. The original inception keeps its year and moves to the new anniversary. The rule outcome is unchanged (`clear`).
- C-016 anniversary: the inception date now falls on the period's anniversary.
- H-016, M-034: the history-outage setup has been removed, both history checks are now `clear`, and `policy_lookup_failed` is no longer allowed. The policy lookup works, so the reason cannot occur.

**Fixed in the checker** (`tools/check_corpus.py`, each with a planted-error self-test)

- The checker recomputes expiry. Quotes now print `valid_until`.
- Weekday-and-date pairs in the input text are checked. All 287 in v1 are correct.
- Document issuers are checked against the corpus business list (`KNOWN_ISSUERS` is now used).
- A schedule's period and excess are compared with the policy that `setup.json` seeds.
- A readable document total over NZD 5,000 requires `amount_over_threshold_or_unknown`.
- Each labelled boundary condition must be carried by the material made for it: blur, keep_pages, truncate, to_tiff, or an unavailable notice.
- Fact-to-document matching uses whole words, so 28 no longer matches inside 128.
- A policy period that starts before the wording's effective date is an error. A period that does not start on the inception anniversary is a warning only; the reviewed 0.2 samples do this.
